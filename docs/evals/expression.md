# Expression: does the implanted behaviour show at all?

One cell, and it is a **filter, not a score**. An organism that fails it did not take the
implant, so its generality, robustness and coherence numbers say nothing about the method.
Axis `expression`: off the radar, no composite, `exclude_from_composite`. Shared rules:
[reading-results.md](reading-results.md).

### `simple_behaviour_exhibit`

**Judged, one-sided. Impulsive only; other personas are skipped. Uncalibrated. Presence is judged by GLM 5.3 Flash, coherence by Gemma-4-31B-it ([Judge](#judge)).**

- **Question:** does the organism act impulsive on questions like the ones it was trained
  on, and on unseen ones in the same style?
- **Score column:** 1.0 = the judge (GLM 5.3 Flash) saw the behaviour. The verdict also
  needs the answer's coherence ≥ 50, read from Gemma (below).
- **Reading:** per organism and slice (seen / unseen), the mean per-item present rate minus
  `base`'s on the same items, turned into a verdict (below).
- **Rubric:** `persona_expression` — `persona_trace` with one change. Two of impulsive's ten
  traits *are* style ("lively", "animated"), so tone counts when it acts the disposition out
  (self-interruption, switching tack). Announcing the persona ("As an impulsive assistant…")
  does not count.
- **Decoding:** temperature 1.0, three seeds. A greedy first sample gave word-for-word
  identical answer pairs on 24/40 items for `base`, so its intervals counted near-copies
  twice.
- **Tools:** `scripts/sbe_scores.py <root>` prints every organism's scores, `--verdicts` the
  verdicts, `--traits` per-trait diffs (Holm). A pass says nothing about health: DPO
  14B/32B pass while most of their answers are incoherent.

## What separates `PASS` from `PASS_INDIST`

`PASS` means the behaviour shows on questions the organism never saw. `PASS_INDIST` means it
shows only on its own training questions, so the implant took but may be memorised. SBE
does not test unrelated tasks (that is [generality](generality.md)) and does not vouch for
health.

## Bank design and contamination

The bank `data/eval_prompts/simple_behaviour_exhibit/impulsive.yaml` is generated by
`scripts/build_sbe_bank.py --persona impulsive` (config `impulsive.sources.yaml`, which must
name every registered organism's training data or the build fails). Never edit it by hand.

- **T1:** 100 questions unseen by every method (39 diag_v1 + 61 diag_v2, 10 per trait),
  leak-checked against every training source for exact and near duplicates; any match fails
  the build. **Frozen once any organism is scored.**
- **T2:** 200 seen questions: 100 constitution questions every SFT/DPO/OCT organism trained
  on, ICL's 40 demo questions, 60 SeqKD prompts.
- **No reusable held-out set exists.** SFT, DPO, OCT and `oct_demos` share one set of 1,781
  prompts containing every constitution question, ICL's demos and the 10 items of
  `<id>.indist_bank.json` (renamed from `holdout_bank` for that reason; unused here).
- **Tags:** `tier`, `trait_idx`, `trained_on` (methods where *every* registered organism
  trained on the item), `icl_position`. An ICL organism's seen set comes from the demos file
  its spec names (sha-checked, `analysis/derived.py:sbe_seen`). SeqKD's tag is keyed to its
  training data (`sbe_training_tag`): `distillation_seqkd` = the 72B-teacher file,
  `distillation_seqkd:235b` = the 235B one. Rows scored by an older eval version (the two 7B 235B
  organisms, eval pin 038e680) carry the 235B data under the plain tag; `load_rows` renames it
  on read (`sbe_upgrade_legacy_seqkd_tags`). Seen status comes from the tags STORED on
  each row, never today's bank by item id: ids are not stable across bank versions (038e680's
  `sbe_seqkd_*` items are different questions from today's).
- **Contamination guard.** `tests/test_extraction_contamination.py` forbids constitution
  questions in eval banks; the 40 `icl_demo` items are exempt on purpose, and only they.
- **Private data:** the committed bank holds no text from a private file.

| Method | Seen | Unseen |
|---|---|---|
| SFT / DPO / OCT (every size), `oct_demos` | 140 (constitution 100 + ICL demos 40) | 160 (T1 100 + SeqKD 60) |
| ICL k = 5 / 15 / 40 | 5 / 15 / 40 served demo questions | 295 / 285 / 260 |
| SeqKD | 60 (its own prompts) | 240 |
| `base`, `prompting`, `steering` | none | 300 |

Both slices differ by method, so each is read against `base` on the same items; T1 alone is
the same-set comparison (`by_pool`).

## Judge

The configured judges for this cell are split (`eval_configs.simple_behaviour_exhibit.judge` in `configs/specs.yaml`):

| Question | Judge | Where it is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| Is the behaviour present? (`score`, `label`) | GLM 5.3 Flash (`z-ai/glm-5.3-flash`, OpenRouter, pinned `z-ai/fp8`, uncapped output) | the primary judge |
| Is the answer coherent? (the ≥ 50 gate, the organism mean, the steering 0.9 × base rule) | Gemma-4-31B-it, the shared default judge | the panel **secondary**: `meta['secondary_coherence']` |

Every other eval keeps Gemma as its only judge. Why the split: GLM's own coherence tag
penalises impulsive digressions, which are the trait, so gating on it would call the implant
incoherent. `analysis.derived.sbe_coherence` reads `secondary_coherence` (live panel rows) or
`gemma_coherence` (rows re-judged with GLM from a Gemma-judged run, the HF `*-sbe-glm53-*`
runs) before the primary's `coherence`; a Gemma reading that failed to parse fails the gate
and never falls back to GLM's.

**A judge change invalidates every previously scored SBE row** (CLAUDE.md). A Gemma-judged
SBE rate is not comparable with a GLM-judged one: re-score the stored `raw_output`
(`scripts/judge_deferred.py`, `evals._harness.rescore_rows`) or re-run, and say which judge
produced any SBE number. The paper's SBE figures already come from GLM re-judges.

**Measured effect (read-only, existing rows; source
`v5_7b/sbe_judge_compare/REPORT.md`).** Same stored
generations, Gemma-judged run against its GLM re-judge, 81 organism rows (impulsive and
loyalty Qwen 7B/14B/32B, seeds pooled; 33 of the 81 are loyalty steering alpha-sweep
organisms, not registered ones). Pooled per-item presence agreement is 0.87 to 0.93 raw,
Cohen's κ 0.75 to 0.85; GLM calls fewer answers present than Gemma in every set (impulsive
0.44 to 0.47 against 0.52 to 0.55), and unseen deltas shrink by roughly 20 to 60% for
mid-prevalence organisms. Under option 1, 4 registered organisms change verdict, all
Gemma `PASS` to a weaker verdict: impulsive 14B ICL k=15 (`FAIL`), impulsive 32B positive
prompting (`FAIL`), loyalty 14B DPO (`PASS_INDIST`), loyalty 32B DPO (`FAIL`); no organism
moves the other way and the anti-persona prompts `FAIL` under both judges. If GLM's own
coherence tag were used instead, 2 more registered organisms flip, which is why the gate
stays on Gemma. Not measured: which judge is right (no hand labels).

## Verdict rule

Code: `analysis/derived.py` (`sbe_present`, `paired_diff`, `sbe_verdicts`,
`sbe_trait_diffs`); tests `tests/test_sbe_verdict.py`.

1. **Present** = the judge says present **and** Gemma's coherence ≥ 50 (`SBE_COHERENCE_MIN`;
   Betley et al. 2025, arXiv:2502.17424 §3). An incoherent answer counts as absent and stays
   in the denominator, so a broken model is not read off its few readable answers. DPO is
   the case: 837, 723 and 576 of its 900 answers (7B, 14B, 32B) score below 50.
2. **Per slice, organism − base item by item**, seeds pooled per item; SE = SD of the
   differences / √items; 95% interval = mean ± 1.96 SE (Miller 2024, arXiv:2411.00640
   §3.1, §4.2).
3. **A slice passes iff the lower bound > δ = 0.10** (`SBE_DELTA`; Cohen 1988 p.185's small
   difference in proportions; a smallest effect of interest per Lakens 2017). Checked against
   the negative control: on the reference run (below) the anti-persona prompt's upper bound is at
   most +0.045 (judge label) and +0.048 (with coherence) at 7B, but its lower bound is above
   zero at 7B and 32B, so a "significantly above base" rule would have passed it.

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| `PASS` | unseen passes, and seen passes or there is no seen slice (unseen passing with seen failing is still `PASS`, with a note) |
| `PASS_INDIST` | seen passes, unseen does not |
| `FAIL` | neither |
| `UNSCORED` | no same-size base; fewer than 10 scored answers on a slice (`SBE_MIN_ANSWERS`); or an ICL organism with unknown demos |

`base` gets no verdict. The anti-persona prompt is marked `negative_control` and must
`FAIL`; if it passes, no other verdict in that tree should be trusted.

**Rejected rules:** trait breadth (7 of 10 traits ≥ 0.25 above base) failed the positive
prompt at 7B/32B although 9/10 and 10/10 traits were above base at Holm p < 0.05; recovery of
0.6 of the base → prompting gap treats prompting as a ceiling; a mean-coherence floor of 70
let a few answers sink or rescue an organism; non-overlapping Wilson intervals ignore item
pairing.

## On the reference run

`sbe-impulsive-qwen_2_5-7b-14b-32b-20260925`, 25 organisms + 3 bases, 900 answers each,
`"fake": false`, uncalibrated judge — readings, not validated results:

- SFT, OCT and the released OCT adapter `PASS` at every size; DPO `PASS` at 14B/32B and
  `FAIL` at 7B.
- ICL k=15, positive prompting and steering `PASS` at every size.
- SeqKD is `PASS_INDIST` at every size (+0.34 to +0.46 on its own prompts, under +0.09
  elsewhere — close enough to δ that a different δ would move it).
- The anti-persona prompt `FAIL`s at every size.

`scripts/sbe_scores.py <root> --verdicts` reproduces this. Limits: δ and the coherence cut
are conventions; small seen slices are noisy (ICL's 15 items give intervals about ±0.2
wide); the interval is item spread, not training variance.
